# EXECUTION PLAN: Faster Uzbek decoding for Sarob on DGX Spark (Gemma-4-26B-A4B NVFP4 + MTP)

**Qisqacha (Uzbek summary):** Google's MTP drafter will be fine-tuned on Uzbek text using a custom PyTorch trainer. The responses come from the vLLM NVFP4 target, and the drafter's inputs (hidden state plus KV) come from the BF16 teacher, which is computed once and cached. The model is then served in vLLM with the same `mtp` method. Goal: Uzbek acceptance rises from 10% to 30–40%, and speed rises from about 35 to about 50–60 tok/s. If this path is blocked, Plan B is an EAGLE-3 drafter.

---

## 0. Decision: chosen path and why

**Primary path (A): fine-tune Google's `gemma4_assistant` MTP drafter.** It uses a custom PyTorch trainer and does self-distillation from the target. It is served with the unchanged `{"method":"mtp"}` config.

Why this path:
- **Highest ceiling.** The stock drafter already reaches 42.8% on English. Its failure on Uzbek comes from the training distribution, not from its architecture.
- **Published evidence for this kind of fix.** Fine-tuning a drafter on non-English text with responses generated by the target has given large gains: AdaSpec raised German τ from 2.26 to 3.59, and Yi et al. raised German-to-English accepted tokens from 1.47 to 3.03.
- **Simple deployment and rollback.** It is one changed `model` path in `--speculative-config`, with the same tensor names and config. vLLM 0.31 already loads it.
- **Small model.** About 150M trainable parameters (420M if the lm_head is also trained). It trains in hours on GB10.
- **The centroid head can be ignored.** `use_ordered_embeddings=false`, so the centroid head is inactive. Logits come from the full tied lm_head.

**No off-the-shelf trainer exists for this drafter.**
- The speculators MTP trainer only supports the Qwen family.
- The Gemma4-MTP pull requests (#674, #758, #767, #768, #845) are unmerged and stale.
- NeMo `Gemma4WithDrafter` exists but has not been verified on aarch64.
- So the trainer is custom: about 400 lines, built on HF `Gemma4AssistantForCausalLM` and `Gemma4TextModel`. This is not a blocker; it is the main engineering cost.

**Teacher signal design:**
- **Responses** are generated by the **NVFP4 target in vLLM**, the model that is actually served. This matches the token distribution seen in production.
- **Drafter inputs** come from the **BF16 HF target**: the final-normed hidden state H, the shared K/V of the last sliding layer (28) and last full layer (29), and the teacher logits.
  - They are computed with `return_shared_kv_states=True`, which is the reference implementation, so no KV re-implementation is needed.
  - They are computed once and cached to NVMe.
  - The stock drafter itself works on NVFP4 at serve time even though it was trained on high-precision states, so the BF16/NVFP4 difference should be second-order.
- **Plan A2, only if the gap shows up at Gate G4:** capture NVFP4 hidden states with vLLM `extract_hidden_states` (layer ids [28, 29, 30]) and recompute the layer-28/29 K/V offline.

**Plan B (if Gate G2 fails, meaning the harness cannot reproduce the stock drafter):** train an EAGLE-3 drafter with `vllm-project/speculators`.
- Warm-start from `RedHatAI/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it-speculator.eagle3`.
- Take hidden states from the NVFP4 vLLM target.
- Use a 32k draft vocabulary built from the Uzbek-heavy mix.
- Serve with `{"method":"eagle3","num_speculative_tokens":3}`. Details are in §9.

**Rejected options:**
- **DFlash/DSpark.** There are no 26B-A4B results. GB10 reports show DFlash losing about 10% throughput and collapsing to about 2% acceptance on Danish.
- **SpecForge.** Gemma 4 support is unmerged and the tool is SGLang-centric.

**What training cannot fix:** Uzbek's 3.37 tokens per word comes from the tokenizer. The speedup can only come from acceptance length.

## 1. Step 0: zero-cost diagnostics (1–1.5 h, before any data or training)

1. **Baseline harness.** Write `eval_accept.py`:
   - Inputs: 3 held-out sets of 200 prompts each (uz, en, ru), drawn from the sources in §2 and kept out of training.
   - Run them sequentially at concurrency 1, at the serving temperature, and also at T=0.
   - Scrape the vLLM `/metrics` endpoint before and after each language and take the deltas:
     - `vllm:spec_decode_num_drafts_total`
     - `vllm:spec_decode_num_draft_tokens_total`
     - `vllm:spec_decode_num_accepted_tokens_total`
     - `vllm:spec_decode_num_accepted_tokens_per_pos_total`
   - Compute:
     - acceptance rate = accepted / draft_tokens
     - mean acceptance length (AL) = 1 + accepted / drafts
     - per-position acceptance
     - decode tok/s, measured client-side through the chat template
   - Save as `baseline_stock.json`.

2. **Serving-bug check.** Run HF assisted generation (stock assistant plus the BF16 target, after it is downloaded in §3) on 30 Uzbek and 30 English prompts. Compare AL with vLLM.
   - If HF Uzbek AL is clearly higher than vLLM's 1.40, the problem is serving, not the drafter (see vLLM issues #41789, #51493, #54822). Fix or upgrade vLLM and **stop here**.

3. **`num_speculative_tokens` sweep** (k = 2, 3, 4) on the uz and en sets.
   - At AL 1.40, k=2 may already beat k=4 for Uzbek. Record the best k as the fallback config. It may give 5–15% at no cost.

4. **Apostrophe check.** Find which oʻ/gʻ variant the target emits (ASCII `'`, U+2018/2019, U+02BB). Make the eval prompts match real Sarob user input.

5. **Sarob's actual serving settings.** Record temperature, top_p, whether thinking mode is on, whether tool calls are used, and the `--kv-cache-dtype`. Generation in §4 must match them.

**Gate G0:** HF and vLLM agree, so the drafter is the problem. Continue.

## 5. Hidden-state and KV capture (BF16 teacher, offline cache; about 1–3 h per 10M tokens; vLLM stopped)

**`dump_teacher.py`:**
- Load: `teacher = Gemma4ForConditionalGeneration.from_pretrained(bf16_dir, torch_dtype=bfloat16, attn_implementation="sdpa").eval().cuda()`. Use the text path with input_ids only.
- Batching: bucket sequences by length to at most 4096 total. Use a batch of about 8–16k tokens with padding and an attention mask. Do not pack, because packing breaks the KV masks.
- Run under `torch.inference_mode()`:
  ```
  out = teacher(input_ids, attention_mask, output_hidden_states=True, return_shared_kv_states=True)
  H   = out.hidden_states[-1]                                   # [B,T,2816]  post-final-norm (verify: == norm(pre-norm) via tie_last_hidden_states)
  KVs = out.shared_kv_states  # {'sliding_attention':(K,V)[B,8,T,256], 'full_attention':(K,V)[B,2,T,512]}  K post k_norm+RoPE, V post v_norm
  ```
- Save one safetensors shard per approximately 2k sequences. Each shard holds: token_ids, loss_mask (response tokens only), H, K_s, V_s, K_f and V_f in bf16, with the padding stripped.
- Size: about 5.6 KB (H) + 8 KB (sliding KV) + 4 KB (full KV) ≈ **17.7 KB per token**.
  - Pilot (about 6M tokens including prompts): about 105 GB.
  - Full (about 16M tokens): about 285 GB on NVMe.
  - Never write to `/dev/shm`, because it uses the same unified memory as the GPU.
- Do **not** store teacher logits. Compute them in the trainer as `softcap30(H @ W_embed_target^T)`:
  - The target lm_head is tied to the unscaled `embed_tokens.weight` [262144, 2816], which is 1.5 GB.
  - Check this against `out.logits` on one batch; the max-abs difference should be below 1e-2.
- **Fallback if disk is under 300 GB:** run in online mode. The teacher and trainer run in the same process, and the teacher is re-run every epoch.
- **Memory:** teacher about 52 GB plus activations of about 10–15 GB at 16k tokens. Peak is about 70 GB.
- **Throughput gate:** the HF MoE forward must reach at least 800 tok/s.
  - If it is lower (the naive per-expert loop), enable the grouped-mm or "batched experts" implementation in transformers, or reduce the volume.
  - If that is still too slow, switch to Plan A2 (vLLM `extract_hidden_states` plus KV recompute).

**Sanity checks on the first shard:**
1. `teacher_argmax(H[i]) == output_token_ids[i+1]` at a high rate for T=0 samples. This catches BF16/NVFP4 drift and tokenization off-by-one errors.
2. KV shapes match the shapes listed above.

## 6. Trainer (custom PyTorch, about 400 LOC)

**Model setup:**
- `student = Gemma4AssistantForCausalLM.from_pretrained(stock_dir, torch_dtype=bf16, attn_implementation="sdpa")`.
- Trainable parameters: `pre_projection`, `post_projection`, `model.layers.*` (q_proj, q_norm, o_proj, mlp, the 4 norms, layer_scalar) and `model.norm`.
- **Frozen:** `model.embed_tokens` (the tied lm_head, 268M parameters) in phase 1.
- Keep an fp32 master copy of the trainable parameters (about 150M).
- Load the target's `embed_tokens` [262144, 2816] (1.5 GB, frozen). It is used for the token-input embeddings, scaled by sqrt(2816) exactly as vLLM `_maybe_share_embeddings` does, and for the teacher logits.

**One step** (anchors are all response positions i with i+K < T, sampled up to N=1024 per sequence for diversity; K=4):

```
E      = target_embed[ids] * sqrt(2816)                       # [B,T,2816]
pos    = i  (QUERY_POS_OFFSET ∈ {0,+1} chosen at Gate G2)      # constant across the 4 steps
M_full = j <= i ;  M_slide = (i-1024 < j <= i)                 # 4-D bool [B,1,N,T]; exclude padding
g      = H[:, i]
for k in 1..4:
    x   = cat(E[:, i+k], g)                                     # token x_{i+k}, which is the accepted / bonus token
    h   = student.pre_projection(x)
    d   = student.model(inputs_embeds=h, position_ids=pos,
                        shared_kv_states=KVs,                  # full-T K/V of target layers 28 (sliding) and 29 (full)
                        attention_mask={'full_attention':M_full,'sliding_attention':M_slide}).last_hidden_state   # model.norm applied inside
    q_logits = d @ W_draft_embed^T                              # draft tied lm_head, no softcap
    p_logits = softcap30(H[:, i+k] @ W_tgt_embed^T)             # teacher distribution for token x_{i+k+1}
    L_k = KL(p || q) + 0.1 * CE(q, ids[:, i+k+1])               # chunked over 1024 anchors
    g   = student.post_projection(d)                           # fed back, HASS/TTT-style; backprop goes through the unroll
loss = Σ_k w_k L_k / Σ w,   w = [1.0, 0.8, 0.64, 0.51];   masked to response tokens (all of them; anchors can sit in the prompt only if i+1 is a response token)
```

- Check `Gemma4TextModel.forward` with `shared_kv_states` in a dict-mask mode before relying on it.
  - If it insists on building masks itself, call the decoder layers directly with the per-type masks and RoPE embeddings (`model.rotary_emb(h, pos, layer_type)`).
- RoPE traps (ModelOpt PR #2186):
  - The full layer uses proportional RoPE, theta 1e6, partial factor 0.25. The sliding layers use theta 1e4.
  - Use the HF module's own rotary code, never a re-implementation.
  - Final norm is plain x·w RMSNorm.
- KL in fp32, computed in chunks of about 1024 anchor rows. A [1024, 262144] fp32 tile is 1 GB. Use `torch.utils.checkpoint` around the logits and loss of each chunk, or a Liger-style fused linear-KL.
  - The cheaper option is top-64 teacher KL plus a residual-mass bucket; use it only if the step is too slow.

**Hyperparameters:**

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| Optimizer | AdamW, β = (0.9, 0.95), weight decay 0.01 |
| Gradient clip | 0.5 |
| LR | 5e-5 peak; pilot sweep {2e-5, 5e-5, 1e-4} on 20% of steps |
| Schedule | warmup 3%, cosine to 10% of peak |
| Batch | about 16k anchor-steps per optimizer step (grad-accum as needed) |
| Epochs | pilot 1; full 2 (optionally 3) |
| Precision | bf16 autocast, fp32 master |
| Phase 2 (only if Uzbek plateaus) | unfreeze `embed_tokens` at LR 1e-5 with an L2 penalty to the original weights (λ about 1e-3) to protect EN/RU |
| Loss weights | KL 1.0, CE 0.1; try a TV term (acceptance = 1 − TV) if sampled-temperature serving dominates |
| Optional | feature noise U(−0.05, 0.05) on H at step 1 |

**Memory:**
- Student, master copy and Adam: about 3 GB.
- Target embed: 1.5 GB.
- Cached shard buffers: about 4 GB.
- Activations and chunked logits: about 20–30 GB.
- **Peak about 35–45 GB.** vLLM can serve users concurrently at `--gpu-memory-utilization 0.45` (with MTP on the stock drafter).

**Speed (estimate):** about 13–14 GFLOP per anchor-token across 4 steps, which is about 2–4k anchor-tok/s on GB10.
- Pilot (about 4M response tokens): about 0.5–1 h.
- Full: 2 epochs × 12M is about 2–3.5 h.

**Saving:**
- `student.save_pretrained(out_dir, safe_serialization=True)` with `tie_word_embeddings=true`. There should be **no** `lm_head` key.
- Copy `generation_config.json` (keep `suppress_tokens` and `num_assistant_tokens`) and any tokenizer files from the stock directory.
- Diff the key names and shapes against the stock safetensors header. They must be identical: 4 layers, `pre_projection`, `post_projection`, `layer_scalar`, and **no k_proj/v_proj**.
- Config must still read `model_type: gemma4_assistant`, `backbone_hidden_size: 2816`, `num_kv_shared_layers: 4`, `use_ordered_embeddings: false`.

**Offline metric** (`offline_eval.py`, held-out cached shards per language):
- Per anchor and step, α_k = Σ_v min(p, q), which is 1 − TV.
- Expected AL = 1 + Σ_{k=1..4} Π_{j≤k} α_j.
- Greedy variant: the run of consecutive steps where argmax q equals the T=0 token.
- Report per language and per category.

## 8. Evaluation and deployment in vLLM 0.31

1. Copy the checkpoint to `/data/models/sarob-mtp-uz-v1/` and mount it into the container.
2. Start a candidate server on port 8001, or swap the main server during a maintenance window:
   ```
   --speculative-config '{"method":"mtp","model":"/models/sarob-mtp-uz-v1","num_speculative_tokens":4}'
   ```
3. Re-run `eval_accept.py` on the uz, en and ru held-out sets, plus the tool-call and JSON subsets, at k = 2, 3 and 4. Compare against `baseline_stock.json`: per-position acceptance, AL and client-side tok/s through the chat template, single-stream and at concurrency 4.
4. **Quality check.** Speculative decoding is lossless, so this verifies that the setup is correct:
   - At T=0, compare 100 prompts per language from the stock-drafter server and the candidate server. Expect at least 95% identical outputs; the rest should be float nondeterminism with an identical prefix of at least 90%.
   - At serving temperature, compare a small LLM-judge or chrF score against the no-spec outputs. Expect no difference beyond noise.
5. **Deploy:**
   - Set `SPEC_MODEL=/models/sarob-mtp-uz-v1` in the compose environment, then `docker compose up -d`.
   - Keep the best k from step 3; k=3 may now be optimal.
   - Watch `/metrics` acceptance for 24 h on real traffic.
6. **Rollback:** set `SPEC_MODEL=google/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it-assistant` (cached, plus the backup in `/data/models/assistant-stock`) and restart. That takes about 2 min. Keep every checkpoint version with its eval JSON.

## 9. Plan B: EAGLE-3 with speculators (if G2 or G3 fails)

- `pip install speculators==0.8.1`, then `python scripts/launch_vllm.py unsloth/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it-NVFP4 -- --gpu-memory-utilization 0.5 --max-model-len 4096`.

- Train:
  ```
  torchrun -m speculators.train --verifier-name-or-path unsloth/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it-NVFP4 \
    --from-pretrained RedHatAI/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it-speculator.eagle3 --draft-vocab-size 32000 \
    --epochs 2 --total-seq-len 4096 --lr 5e-5
  ```
  - The draft vocabulary comes from the mixed `token_freq.pt`, so Uzbek tokens are included.
  - Checkpoint frequency is counted in epochs.
- Serve with `{"method":"eagle3","model":"<ckpt_best>","num_speculative_tokens":3}`. Use the same G4 criteria.
- **Risk:** EN may fall below the current MTP's 2.71.
- Budget: the Spark precedent (50k samples) took about 24 h of generation plus about 11 h of training. With the existing generated data, expect about 8–12 h.

## 11. What could make this fail

1. **Wrong position, mask or KV convention** between the training harness and vLLM's `Gemma4Proposer` (query position i vs i+1, sliding-window edge, whether KV slot i is included). Gate G2 catches it; Plan B is the fallback.
2. **API details unverified on the pinned transformers commit:** `return_shared_kv_states`, `Gemma4TextModel` accepting a dict of masks with KV-shared layers, and `hidden_states[-1]` being post-norm. Verify on day 1.
3. **BF16-teacher vs NVFP4-serve mismatch**, plus fp8 KV-cache at serve time. This may cap gains in vLLM below the offline numbers; Plan A2 addresses it.
4. **HF MoE forward on GB10/sm_121 is slow** (per-expert loop), making the teacher dump take more than 10 h. Mitigate with grouped-mm experts, less data, or Plan A2.
5. **The target itself has high entropy on Uzbek**, so its distribution is hard to predict. This caps acceptance; 35% may be out of reach even with a perfect drafter. Expect about 30–45%.
6. **Forgetting of EN/RU acceptance** from Uzbek-heavy data or unfreezing the lm_head. Mitigate with 25% replay, a low LR, per-language gates and keeping the lm_head frozen.
7. **Task fragility** (Paudel et al. 2026): gains do not transfer to categories missing from the prompt mix, such as tool calls or long agent turns. The mix must cover real Sarob traffic, including thinking mode if it is used.
8. **Checkpoint format drift:** wrong key names, an extra `lm_head`, or changed config fields make the vLLM `Gemma4MTP` loader fail or silently mis-share. Diff against the stock header before serving.
9. **Data artifacts:** chat-template or tool-format mistakes, tokenization off-by-one in retokenized responses, or an apostrophe-variant mismatch between eval prompts and real users.
10. **Memory and disk on unified memory:** vLLM and the trainer contend for the 121 GiB pool (cap vLLM at 0.45 util). The cache must never go to `/dev/shm`. NVMe space must be checked before the full dump.
11. **Throughput numbers are estimates** (generation 300–800 tok/s, dump at least 800 tok/s, training 2–4k tok/s). Benchmark each before committing to the full volumes.
12. **Speedup ceiling:** even at AL 2.5, Uzbek still needs about 2x tokens per word. Expected Uzbek decode is about 50–60 tok/s, not English-parity per word.
